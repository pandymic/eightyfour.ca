Parapit

Cross the wall. Don’t fall.

Watch Parapit in motionJoin beta test

Keep your footing

A storm is breaking over the gorge, and the academy’s entrance trial won’t wait for it to pass. The task is simple. Run the length of a narrow, crumbling stone wall, high above the valley, and reach the tower at the far end. Most runners decide how fast you go. This one hands you the throttle, and the speed you pick is the whole game.

  • Balance is your health. There’s no health bar. Where you stand on the walkway is how close you are to falling.
  • You set the pace. Too slow and the wind shoves you back. Too fast and you slide on wet stone.
  • Every jump is the same arc, so you learn exactly how far it reaches. The gaps test whether you did.
  • Every wall is built from a seed. Share one with a friend and race the same stones.
  • Two modes: Adventure, to the tower at your own pace, and Runner, an endless wall at a flat-out sprint.
  • Three difficulties, counted in boots: two, one, or barefoot.
  • Learn as you go. A training wall teaches each hazard one at a time, and tips pop up the first times you meet something.

On the wall

One thumb sets your speed. The other jumps and ducks. Hold a steady stride and you drift back to the middle of the walkway, where the next shove can go either way. Dawdle at a ledge and it crumbles under you. Sprint through a puddle and your feet go out.

There’s no progress bar. Watch the tower grow in the distance, and the banners bunch up along the wall as you get close.

Learn the wall

New to the wall? The training wall teaches it one thing at a time. Run, find your stride, leap a gap, cross wet stone and a crumbling ledge. Then duck a bat, hop a dropped pack, get past a stumbling cadet, vault a charger, and duck the one that turns back to lunge. Each lesson waits until you’ve done it, and a fall only replays that lesson.

On a real wall, short tips pop up the first couple of times you meet something. The first time something needs a quick reaction, the storm slows for a moment so you can read it. How to play has illustrated pages on the controls and every hazard. Know it all already? One switch turns the hints off.

Balance is your health

Every knock pushes you toward one edge of the walkway. Reach it and you go over. The game tells you how bad it is without a single number on screen.

  • You wobble. The worse your balance, the more the runner sways.
  • The screen darkens from the side you’re leaning over, and the view drifts toward the drop.
  • You hear it. Grit under your feet first, then your own heartbeat, faster and faster.
  • Your phone buzzes at the brink. One more push and you’re gone.
  • A steady stride fixes it. Find the pace, hold it, and you drift back to the centre.

The wall fights back

  • Gaps and ledges

    Broken stone to leap, and ledges that give way if you dawdle. Near the edge, the stones start falling from under your feet.

  • Bats

    They swoop in at head height. Duck, and stay down until they’ve passed.

  • Chargers

    Rival cadets sprint up from behind to shove you off. Red arrows warn you. Jump them as they pass. Some double back, brace and lunge: watch for the red “!”.

  • Stumblers

    Struggling cadets ahead who might topple at any moment and take you with them. Vault them or give them room.

  • Packs and boots

    Somebody’s dropped backpack, somebody’s lost boot. Hop them, or nearly go over.

  • Gusts

    You’ll hear the wind swell before it hits. It shoves you back, and it brings branches and torn banners in at head height.

The arrows flash red at the edge of the screen. That’s a charger, coming up fast behind you. One touch and you’re over the edge. They can fall too, though, and two that collide knock each other off.

A living storm

Rain drives across the wall the whole way. Lightning splits the sky over the crags, and each flash lights up the dragons flying far off in the dark. Then count to the thunder. When it rolls in, the stones shake, and running while they shake is risky.

If flashes bother you, switch on Reduce flashing. Screen shake and haptics have their own switches, and every sound has its own volume.

Two ways to play

  • Adventure

    Cross the wall to the tower at your own pace. Type in a seed to run a wall again, or leave it blank for a new one every time.

  • Runner

    An endless wall. She sets off at a canter and is sprinting flat out within a minute, faster than Adventure ever allows. Tap to jump, swipe to slide. Your score is how far you got.

Choose your boots

Difficulty is how many boots she runs in, and you can see it on her feet. Lace up both for good grip and a shorter wall. Lose one, or go barefoot, and the wall gets longer, the walkway narrower, the stone slicker, and there’s more of everything trying to knock you off.

Not sure you can make it? Flip a switch and watch the autopilot run the wall first. Another turns the hints on or off.

Run it again

  • Save any run as a replay, whether you made it or not. Watch it back at up to four times the speed.
  • Race your ghosts. Run the same wall against up to three of your saved runs, shown as grey ghosts on their own paths.
  • Replays are tiny. They store your inputs, not a video, and they stay on your phone.
  • Left-handed? One setting swaps the two halves of the screen.
  • Chunky pixel art on a voxel world, in a 2.5D view that shows you just how narrow the walkway is.

Help test Parapit

Parapit is in closed testing on Google Play. Testers play each new build before it’s out, and tell me where the wall felt unfair, too easy or too long.

  • What you need: an Android phone or tablet, and the Google account you use on Google Play.
  • Ask to join with the button below. I add your account to the test and email you the link to accept; it can take a few days.
  • Install Parapit from Google Play as usual once you’ve accepted. New test versions arrive as ordinary updates.
  • Leave any time with “Leave the program” on the test’s Google Play page.
